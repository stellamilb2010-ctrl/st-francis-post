The Odyssey: A Modern Take on an Ancient Epic

By Rafaela Agudo · September 16, 2025

The Odyssey directed by Christopher Nolan, a famous film director, screenwriter, and film producer, was released on July 17 this year by Universal Pictures and depicts a modern take on ancient Greece. The film reached 12,800 theatrical locations and 25,600 screens worldwide, and starred a quite prestigious cast such as Matt Damon, Tom Holland, Anne Hathaway, Robert Pattinson, Lupita Nyong'o, Zendaya, and Charlize Theron.

The type of camera used was Imax, an ultra high resolution camera system that utilises a massive 65mm film frame running horizontally, that is ten times larger than a standard film frame of 35mm, and was very helpful filming massive scale scenes. That made the Odyssey become the first feature film shot entirely by this new fascinating camera film technology, becoming a revolutionising cinematic trademark.

In the film, and any other of the directors pieces, Nolan has a keen obsession on minimising the use of Computer Generated Imagery (CGI), and instead relies on more practical effects and physical features, creating even more drama and action to the movie. That helps make the film more real and we connect more deeply to the main character, Odyssey done by outstanding actor Matt Damon.

The film's main plot is about a Greek king and warrior Odyssey (Matt Damon) that is on an epic journey to return to his wife in Ithaca, an island in western Greece after the deadly Trojan war. In his journey he has to overcome Greek mystical creatures such as the famous cyclops and the gods are not in his favour, making his journey even more difficult. Although many of the gods are represented by a physical character, in the movie such as Athena Circe and Calypso, the gods Zeus Poseidon and Helios are represented through animals and symbolic forces of nature transcending their natural power. The mighty goddess Athena helps guide Odyssey and navigate through his thoughts. Odysseus has to pass through various struggles fighting mystical creatures and daring situations.

This film is emotionally pragmatic and the acting very well done and believable making the character feel real and more alive with very good cinematic effects along with a great choice of using IMAX as the film camera. The story was extremely interesting as it highlighted very well the power of the gods and something I enjoyed was the deep connection they manage to showcase of Odyssey with his wife and his emotional trauma of the Trojan war.

The film soundtrack, composed by Ludwig Göransson, is extremely well crafted and is a great choice for the drama as it has high energetic notes used in more intense moments such as the battle scenes and lower softer notes, used during the melancholic moments such as of Penelope with her son, mourning the loss of Ithaca seeing their kingdom fall and crumple, something sad and emotionally compelling.

Though my main criticism is that the film did not depict a lot of female goddesses, particularly Athena and other female figures. The film is heavily man driven, and the goddess Athena was not as heightened not being so true to its main story line. Their role was not as developed compared with the male characters and was limited, even though in the Greek mythology Athena is the most powerful goddess in the Odyssey. Athena could have appeared more and could have had a bigger role than what was showcased in the film.

Overall even though the film did lack some parts, it was extremely well done with great cinematic effects and all the actors did manage to be believable, making the film a great depiction of an outstanding plot, and showcasing the deep emotional parts of greek mythology and all the characters, internal turmoil and struggle.

So yes I do recommend this film and the 3 hours of movie I promise will not bore you at all, making you hold your breath of tension during the entire plot.
